# Content Authoring Rules

## Core principle

A question is not valid merely because its wording sounds educational. It must
measure the mapped standard, have one defensible scoring rule, be developmentally
appropriate, and survive automated validation plus adult review.

## Required template review

A reviewer must confirm:

1. The official standard really supports the question.
2. The question measures the intended skill rather than unrelated reading,
   vocabulary, motor speed, or cultural knowledge.
3. The canonical answer is unambiguous.
4. Distractors are distinct and represent plausible misconceptions.
5. The explanation teaches the reasoning.
6. Numbers, words, and visuals remain valid across every generated seed.
7. The question is usable without color alone.
8. Pre-reading questions have audio-capable instructions.
9. Source and licensing provenance are complete.
10. No private child data appears in prompts or content.

## ELA rules

- Use original passages unless a passage is unmistakably reusable and documented.
- Early phonics items must control the spelling pattern being tested.
- Do not introduce irregular words into a decoding item unless irregularity is the
  target.
- Distinguish phonemic awareness, which can be entirely spoken, from phonics, which
  connects sounds to print.
- A comprehension question should not require outside knowledge to answer unless the
  standard explicitly targets integrating knowledge.
- For text evidence questions, preserve the relevant sentence/paragraph offsets.

## Math rules

- Prefer exact arithmetic and rational representations over floating-point equality.
- Store fractions as numerator/denominator values.
- Every visual model must match the mathematical whole.
- Generated word problems must use sensible units and quantities.
- Distractors should reflect likely errors such as reversal, off-by-one counting,
  place-value confusion, operation confusion, or denominator mistakes.
- Never create impossible real-world quantities merely to randomize a problem.
- Do not require speed for conceptual standards.

## Difficulty

Difficulty is not simply a larger number. It may vary through:

- number range;
- number of steps;
- visual support;
- distractor similarity;
- vocabulary complexity;
- scaffolding;
- whether the answer is selected or constructed;
- whether representations are familiar or transferred.

Record the chosen dimensions rather than only a single opaque difficulty score.

## Feedback

After submission:

- acknowledge effort neutrally;
- state whether the answer is correct;
- show or play the key reasoning;
- allow another example at a slightly easier level after repeated misses;
- do not call the learner lazy, careless, bad, or behind;
- do not remove previously earned mastery rewards.
